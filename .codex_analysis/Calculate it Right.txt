Loss  Source 
Typical  Loss 
Explanation 
Temperature  losses 5  to  10% 
Panels  lose  approximately  0.4%  of  rated  power  per  degree  C  above  25  degrees  C.  Nigerian  roof  temperatures  regularly  reach  60  to  70  degrees  C,  causing  significant  losses. 
Dust  and  soiling 2  to  5% 
Dust,  bird  droppings,  and  pollen  on  the  panel  surface  reduce  light  reaching  the  cells.  Harmattan  season  makes  this  worse. 
Inverter  conversion 3  to  5% 
Converting  DC  electricity  from  panels  and  batteries  to  AC  electricity  for  appliances  is  not  100%  efficient.  Typical  inverters  are  88  to  95%  efficient. 
Cable  resistance 1  to  3% 
All  cables  have  electrical  resistance.  Current  flowing  through  resistance  generates  heat,  which  is  wasted  energy.

 The  duty  cycle  values  used  throughout  this  book  are: •  Refrigerators:  50%  duty  cycle  (compressor  runs  approximately  half  of  stated  
time) •  Deep  Freezers:  40%  duty  cycle  (colder  setting  means  less  compressor  
running
 
time) •  Air  Conditioners:  80%  duty  cycle  (cycles  less  frequently,  mostly  runs  when  
switched
 
on) •  All  other  appliances  (fans,  lights,  TV,  laptop,  pump):  100%  duty  cycle  
TIP:  Conservative  vs  Accurate  Approach Using  duty  cycle  gives  you  a  more  accurate  and  lower  load  figure,  which  correctly  sizes  the  system  and  saves  your  client  money.  If  you  prefer  a  conservative  approach  with  extra  safety  buffer,  you  can  ignore  duty  cycle  and  calculate  full  stated  hours.  This  oversizes  the  system  by  20  to  50%  but  ensures  the  client  never  runs  short.  Always  tell  the  client  which  approach  you  used  so  they  understand  the  sizing.  
PRO  INSTALLER  TIP Always  ask  your  client  how  they  actually  use  each  appliance,  not  how  long  they  leave  it  switched  on.  A  client  who  leaves  the  refrigerator  running  24  hours  may  actually  open  it  rarely,  meaning  the  compressor  runs  less  than  40%  of  the  time.  A  client  who  opens  the  fridge  constantly  may  see  60%  duty  cycle.  These  details  matter  for  accurate  large  system  design.  
1.5   Key  Takeaways  and  Practice  Exercises  KEY  TAKEAWAYS 
  Watts  measures  power  at  any  moment.  Watt-Hours  measures  energy  consumed  over  time  Energy  (Wh)  =  Power  (W)  x  Time  (Hours).  Always  multiply  by  time,  never  use  watts  alone   Africa  averages  5  Peak  Sun  Hours  per  day.
